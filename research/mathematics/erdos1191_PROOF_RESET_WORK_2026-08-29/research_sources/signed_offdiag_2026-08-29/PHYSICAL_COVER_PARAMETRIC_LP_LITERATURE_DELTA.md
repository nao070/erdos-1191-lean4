# Physical-cover parametric-LP and mixed-scale Haar literature delta

Search date: 2026-08-30 (Asia/Tokyo)

## Exact interface searched

The new Route-C probes hold one finite Golomb history fixed and vary a Haar
width `T`.  Half-open physical-cell endpoints occur when a translated Haar
endpoint meets a physical point, so the combinatorial cell arrangement changes
only at explicit values `T=d` or `T=d/2`.  Within each resulting open chamber,
the root costs and cell lengths are rational functions of `T`, and the finite
cover LP can be certified by exact primal and dual rational data.

The search asked two narrow questions:

1. Is exact one-parameter LP solution by parameter intervals/basis changes a
   known general method?
2. Is there a directly matching published theorem for the oriented inner
   product of two translated, unequal-width Haar dipoles together with the
   actual-cell cover-excess identity used here?

Exa, Firecrawl, Consensus, and SciSpace were used for discovery.  Consensus
reported that its monthly search quota was exhausted.  Discovery snippets were
not treated as theorem evidence; the substantive records below are primary
publisher pages.

## Primary precedents

### Väliaho: one-parametric LP by explicit parameter dependence

Hannu Väliaho, *A procedure for one-parametric linear programming*, BIT
Numerical Mathematics 19 (1979), 256--269,
[DOI 10.1007/BF01930856](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01930856).

The publisher abstract states that all LP coefficients may be linear or
polynomial functions of one scalar parameter and that the solution vector and
optimal value are determined explicitly as rational functions of that
parameter.  This is a direct precedent for treating the Route-C finite cover as
a one-parameter exact LP and for expecting basis changes at finitely many
critical parameter values.  It does not contain the Sidon/Haar cell geometry,
the project-specific breakpoint set, or any of the certified chamber values.

### Adelgren: a current uni-parametric optimization algorithm

Nathan Adelgren, *A unified tool for solving uni-parametric linear programs,
convex quadratic programs, and linear complementarity problems*, Optimization
Letters 20 (2026), 1103--1119,
[DOI 10.1007/s11590-025-02277-3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11590-025-02277-3).

The primary publisher record allows one parameter in any input data and gives a
general computational technique for uni-parametric LP/QP/LCP instances.  This
confirms that parametric active-set or pivot tracking is established
methodology.  The Route-C certificate instead enumerates its geometry-derived
events and verifies every displayed primal/dual identity directly over the
rationals; it does not invoke Adelgren's algorithm as a proof step.

### Kolev--Skalna is a different interval-hull problem

Lubomir V. Kolev and Iwona Skalna, *Exact solution to a parametric linear
programming problem*, Numerical Algorithms 78 (2018), 1183--1194,
[DOI 10.1007/s11075-017-0418-6](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11075-017-0418-6).

Despite the title, the publisher abstract concerns an interval hull over a
parameter box, with possibly nonlinear coefficient functions and an iterative
interval-parametric linear-system method.  It is not the same claim as an
exact chamber decomposition of the Route-C physical-cover LP.  It is therefore
recorded as a useful exclusion, not supporting evidence for the certificate.

## Mixed-scale Haar retrieval result

Targeted searches for translated unequal-width Haar autocorrelation did not
surface a primary paper matching the exact oriented formula and actual-cell
cover theorem used in the new probe.  The formula itself is elementary and is
proved in the project by partitioning the five shifted half-open endpoints;
the cover-excess identity is likewise a finite integral over those atomic
cells.  The absence of a retrieved match is only a dated search result.  It is
not evidence of novelty or of absence from the literature.

## Limited synthesis and publication boundary

The general parametric-LP architecture is known.  What is project-specific is
the exact physical event list, the rational chamber certificates, and their
interaction with the direct-`B` Haar/Gothic ownership ledger.  Conversely, the
mixed-scale correlation and cell-excess identities are elementary enough that
they should be presented as direct lemmas, not marketed as new optimization
theory.

No source located here supplies a compatible infinite Sidon history, the
finite-horizon signed payment theorem C058, or either answer to Erdős Problem
#1191.  The search is not saturated: the existing research state still has an
unsaturated citation-chase route, and the targeted Haar query returned no
matching paper rather than a theorem of absence.  Publication novelty, both
Erdős questions, and every prize claim remain open.
