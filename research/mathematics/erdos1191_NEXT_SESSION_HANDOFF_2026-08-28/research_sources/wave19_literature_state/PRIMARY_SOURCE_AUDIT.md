## Direct results and limits

| Primary source | Exact checked result | Mapping to P23 |
|---|---|---|
| Alexeev--Mixon, [arXiv:2510.19804v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.19804) | Theorems 8 and 9 exhibit finite Sidon sets that do not extend to finite cyclic perfect difference sets. Claim 10 states that every finite integer Sidon set extends to an infinite perfect difference set by greedily inserting a pair realizing each missing difference. The paper also notes that an already infinite Sidon set need not extend. | **Direct but qualitative.** Claim 10 is a genuine extension/birth statement, but it gives no bound on the inserted location, preserves no ordered infinite branch, and supplies no density, dyadic-delay, or cumulative-cost estimate. The infinite nonextension example prevents promotion of finite extendability to a fixed-branch completion theorem. |
| Ruzsa, [DOI 10.1556/sscmath.38.2001.1-4.27](https://doi.org/10.1556/sscmath.38.2001.1-4.27) | The accessible theorem statement gives a real `alpha` and `n0` for which `{n^5+floor(alpha n^4): n>n0}` is Sidon. Full proof text was not accessible. | **Directly inapplicable at the target scale.** Its ordered elements grow as `n^5`, far beyond `C n^2 log(2n)`. This is a scoped negative comparison, not a deep-read result. |
| Cilleruelo, [arXiv:1209.0326v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1209.0326) | Theorem 1.1 gives an explicit infinite Sidon set with counting exponent `(3-sqrt(5))/2`. Theorem 1.2, after deleting bad primes, gives exponent `sqrt(2)-1`. Proposition 2 constrains the two construction scales in a repeated-sum collision by `(1-c)k_1^2 < k_2^2 < c k_1^2/(1-c)` and congruence conditions. Theorem 1.3 extends the scheme to `B_h`. | **Direct construction, insufficient growth.** Even the stronger exponent `c=sqrt(2)-1` gives inverse growth `a_n=n^(1+sqrt(2)+o(1))`, worse than the P23 hypothesis. Proposition 2 controls collisions only inside this engineered mixed-radix construction; it is not a dyadic upcrossing or birth-time theorem for an arbitrary fixed critical branch. |
| Cilleruelo--Nathanson, [arXiv:math/0609244v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0609244) | Theorem 1 embeds the counting-function shadow of any Sidon sequence `B` into a perfect difference set `A`, with `A(x) >= B(x/3)-omega(x)`. The construction scales and prunes `B`, then realizes each missing difference `k` by a pair near `4^{g(k)}`, where `g` may grow arbitrarily fast. Theorem 3 gives `limsup A(x)/sqrt(x) >= 1/sqrt(2)`. Section 4.1 explicitly calls the witness sequence very irregular and says the method gives a poor upper bound. | **Closest direct birth construction, quantitatively insufficient.** The arbitrary `g(k)` provides no birth-delay bound; scaling and pruning destroy prescribed-prefix survival; the density conclusion is only `limsup`. The authors' own open question isolates the missing quantitative witness control. |
| Riblet, [arXiv:2202.01296v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.01296) | Theorem 2.1 finds a Sidon subset of size at least `(0.876-o(1))sqrt(n)` in any union of two intervals of total size `n`. Theorem 3.1 supplies upper bounds for Sidon subsets of unions of `k` intervals, proved using short windows, Cauchy--Schwarz, and uniqueness of small differences. | **Finite geometric analogy.** These are genuine window-packing estimates, but they retain only finite cardinality. They do not track order, nested prefixes, signs, first-birth labels, or terminal-to-descendant genealogy, so they do not yield fixed-branch dyadic upcrossing control. |
| Carter--Hunter--O'Bryant, [arXiv:2310.20032v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20032) | Theorem 1.1 is the exact Erdős--Turán Sidon Set Equality, expressing finite diameter through missing-difference slack `S(A,T)` and sliding-window variance `V(A,T)`. Corollary 1.2 converts a lower bound of order `k^(5/2)` for `V` at `T` of order `k^(3/2)` into an improved diameter coefficient. Lemmas 3.1--3.2 combine multiple windows; the proof of Theorem 3.3 gives `diam(A) >= k^2-1.96365 k^(3/2)-O(k)`. The theorem display has an apparent sign typo, while the abstract, introduction, and proof agree on the lower bound. | **Closest exact quantitative theorem.** It provides finite multiscale endpoint energy, but applying it separately to prefixes does not control a nested history. `S` and `V` do not encode first births or the signed carrier/cross-ratio cancellations required to absorb terminal-to-descendant rectangles. Thus it is a plausible ingredient, not a closure of P23. |
| Balasubramanian--Dutta, [DOI 10.1016/j.jnt.2025.07.007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jnt.2025.07.007), primary preprint [arXiv:2409.01986v4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01986) | For a near-extremal finite Sidon set in `[n]`, Theorem 3 proves `a_m=m sqrt(n)+O(n^(7/8))+O(L^(1/2)n^(3/4))`, using the uniform interval discrepancy estimate stated as Theorem A. The paper notes that this is asymptotically informative mainly for `m` near `sqrt(n)`. | **Direct finite coordinate regularity, hypothesis mismatch.** A prefix satisfying only `a_n <= C n^2 log(2n)` need not be near extremal in its ambient interval. The error scale does not localize first births, and the theorem gives no dyadic-upcrossing, carrier, or signed multiscale estimate. |

## Adjacent product-geometry correction

For Martikainen's [arXiv:2608.22628v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22628), the current v1 numbering of the two relevant positive packing results is **Theorem 1.3** (critical two-depth bi-parameter packing) and **Theorem 1.11** (the Zygmund-boundary analogue), not Theorems 1.1 and 1.2. These are sharp geometric templates only: they contain no Sidon/Golomb branch, arithmetic box profile, first-birth labels, or summably vanishing fixed-ray constant.
